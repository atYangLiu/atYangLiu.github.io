<h3>Corporate climate accounting boundaries: The case of AI carbon removal procurement</h3>
<p class="paper-authors">Yang Liu and Gal Hochman</p>
<p>Using global carbon dioxide removal procurement records from 2020–2025, we examine how AI companies’ purchasing patterns and carbon accounting boundaries shape the perceived scale of corporate climate action.</p>
<p class="research-links"><a class="paper-download" href="{{ '/files/Yang_Liu_JMP.pdf' | relative_url }}"><i class="far fa-file-pdf" aria-hidden="true"></i> Read paper (PDF)</a></p>
<details class="paper-abstract">
<summary>Abstract</summary>
<p>Artificial intelligence (AI) companies are aggressively expanding their physical infrastructure, relying on voluntary carbon dioxide removal (CDR) markets to offset their growing environmental footprints. Analysis of global CDR procurement records from 2020 to 2025 reveals contrasting participation and procurement patterns across AI business types, alongside severe structural vulnerabilities in corporate climate governance: extreme market monopsony, the masking of unchecked operational emissions growth, and a Scope 3 illusion where discretionary reporting boundaries drastically inflate the perceived scale of corporate climate action. Aligning voluntary CDR markets with genuine climate mitigation requires mandatory full-value-chain reporting and a strict decoupling of forward purchase commitments from delivered net removals.</p>
